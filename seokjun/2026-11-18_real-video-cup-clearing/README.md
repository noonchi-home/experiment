# Real Video, Cup Clearing — Activity Chunks and the Adoption Test (2026-11-18)

## Summary

I turned 4 real videos, recorded by teammate Junhyeong Kim at my request, into text, appended them after synthetic scenes, and ran the adoption test on logs of about 5 minutes per day × 4 days. Using the per-second log as it is, 2 of 4 robot tasks came out right; grouping the log into activity chunks first gave 3 of 4.

## Why I ran this experiment

- Until now, the log sentences were generated by code. In practice, an AI watches the video and writes the sentences. The biggest open question was whether signals and actions survive in those sentences too.
- So I wanted to turn one real video scene into text and see whether the nightly steps (self-reflection → timestamp finding → adoption test) still produce correct rules from that text.

## What I did

**Real videos**

- 4 real videos recorded by teammate Junhyeong Kim at my request. The same person did the same thing at the same desk four times: works while drinking water, empties the cup, and a little later carries the empty cup out of frame and comes back empty-handed.
- Filmed with a single fixed phone (portrait, 1080×1920, 30 fps). Lengths range from 53 seconds to 1 minute 35 seconds.
- The videos were transcribed by AI at one line per second, then reviewed and finalized by hand. For the transcription, following the paper's settings, the AI looked at the 5 most recent frames (2 seconds apart) and described the scene in one sentence. Writing every 2 seconds would miss the moment of standing up with the cup between frames, so I wrote one line per second, as in the paper.
- I checked the answer key against the videos. For each video it records when the person took a sip, took the last sip, put the empty cup down, picked up the cup, left, and came back empty-handed. It is not part of the AI's input.

**Four days of daily logs**

- Each day is about 5 minutes of evening, with one line per second, so 277–298 lines per day.
- Flow: eating a meal → leaving with the empty bowl and coming back with canned coffee → drinking the coffee → leaving with the empty can and coming back → studying (real video; day N = video N).
- The first part (the meal and the canned coffee) is a synthetic scene in the same style. For each second, a fragment was freshly picked from sentences the AI wrote while watching the real videos and stitched in. The AI was not called for this.
- Traps: a sip of water during the meal, the phone, stretching, and leaving the seat three times (bowl, can, glass).
- Signal to action: finished eating → picked up the bowl, 13–28 seconds; finished the coffee → picked up the can, 17–31 seconds; put the empty glass down → picked up the glass, 20–52 seconds.
- 4 robot tasks: bowl into the dishwasher, can next to the person, can to its storage place, cup into the dishwasher.
- The test window is 1 minute and the pass threshold is 75%. Since a day is only 5 minutes long, a 5-minute window would count everything in the day as "followed."

**Chunked logs**

- The per-second log keeps mentioning what was just done for a few more lines ("finished drinking and put the empty can down, and is now typing"). If the timestamp-finding AI counts such lines as new occurrences of the same moment, the earlier occurrences become "not followed" in the test and the signal fails.
- So I had the AI first group each day's log into a chunked log (the per-second log grouped into units of "what the person is doing"). The 277–298 lines per day became 38–48 chunks.
- All the key moments in the answer key (finished eating, picked up the bowl, finished the coffee, picked up the empty can, put the empty glass down, picked up the glass, came back empty-handed) fell on chunk boundaries (exactly or within 1 second).

**Nightly steps**

I ran the same 4 days twice: once on the per-second log as it is (without chunks), and once on the chunked log. The AI is Qwen3.5-27B.

## Results

Robot rules after night 4

| Robot task | Correct signal | Without chunks | With chunks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bowl into the dishwasher | Finished eating | Right: "has finished eating, with an empty bowl" 4 of 4, and others | Right: "eats the last spoonful" 4 of 4; "has finished eating and sits with both hands at rest" 3 of 3 |
| Empty can to its storage place | Finished the coffee | Wrong: the same moment was counted several times, so all failed (4 of 18, 4 of 7, 4 of 16) | Right: "puts the empty coffee can down on the desk" 3 of 3; "types on the laptop with the empty can nearby" 3 of 4 |
| Cup into the dishwasher (real video) | Finished the water | Right: "typing with the empty glass nearby" 4 of 5 | Right: "types on the laptop with the empty glass mug nearby" 4 of 4 |
| Can next to the person (bringing the coffee) | Finished eating | Wrong: no rule | Wrong: a rule exists ("the last spoonful"), but the timestamp-finding AI never found the action |

- Without chunks, 2 of 4 were right; with chunks, 3 of 4 (bowl, empty can, glass).
- Candidates the test blocked (with chunks): "looks at the phone with the empty bowl nearby" (1 of 1) and "looks at the phone with the empty glass nearby" (1 of 1) appeared on only one day and were blocked by the chance probability (p-value). "Comes back to the desk empty-handed" → clearing the cup was 1 of 5, so it was blocked because the action did not follow.
- All 12 action times the timestamp-finding AI pinpointed (3 tasks × 4 days) matched the answer key exactly or within 1 second.

## What I found digging into the results

1. **The empty can failed because the same moment was counted several times, then passed with chunks.** In reality, the coffee is finished once a day. Without chunks, the empty-can candidate passed on night 2 at 2 of 2, but failed on night 3 at 3 of 5–6, and on night 4 stood at 4 of 18, 4 of 7, and 4 of 16. After chunking, it passed on night 4 at 3 of 3 and 3 of 4.
2. **The real-video part (the glass) was right both with and without chunks.** Even in sentences transcribed from video, the signal of working with the empty glass nearby passed as a rule, and the time the glass was picked up matched the answer key exactly or within 1 second on all 4 days.
3. **The timestamp-finding AI never found the coffee delivery in all four days.** The log contains "sits down at the desk and puts the coffee can on the desk," but the AI did not treat this as "putting the can next to the person." My guess is that this is because the can is taken out off-screen. Timestamp finding ran with thinking turned off, so there is no record of its reasoning.

## Limitations and next steps

- With 4 videos and 5 minutes per day, this is a small test. Most of each day's log is synthetic.
- The timestamp-finding AI never found the coffee delivery in all four days.
- Chunked logs and the adoption test (the action follows within 5 minutes of the signal at least 75% of the time, with a chance probability below 0.1%) went into habit learning in the final product (noonchi).
